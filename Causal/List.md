| **Keywords** | causal inference, Bayesian networks, causal discovery, structural equation models, latent variables, d-separation, causal indistinguishability, manipulation and intervention, regression and causation, experimental and observational studies |

**Abstract**:

Causation, Prediction, and Search addresses how observations can be turned into causal knowledge, and how incomplete causal knowledge can be used in planning, prediction, and control. The authors use the formalism of directed graphical models (Bayesian networks) to axiomatize the connection between causal structure and probabilistic independence. The book develops the mathematical and conceptual framework for representing causal systems, characterizing when alternative causal theories are statistically indistinguishable, and designing computationally feasible search algorithms that can recover causal structure from observational and experimental data. It covers discovery algorithms for causally sufficient and insufficient structures, prediction of manipulations and interventions, the relationship between regression and causation, and the design of empirical studies. The second edition adds a new introduction, a revised treatment of d-separation, and an extensive survey chapter reviewing advances and applications since the first edition (1993).

This book provides a comprehensive exposition of modern causal analysis, showing how causality has developed from an intuitive concept into a mathematical theory with applications in statistics, artificial intelligence, economics, philosophy, cognitive science, and the health and social sciences. | **Keywords** | causal inference, structural causal models, graphical models, d-separation, interventions, backdoor criterion, front-door criterion, inverse probability weighting, mediation, counterfactuals, confounding |

**Abstract**:

A concise introductory primer on causal inference aimed at statistics students and educators. It begins with preliminaries in probability, statistics, and graphs, then introduces structural causal models. The book explains how causal assumptions are encoded in directed graphs and tested against data via chains, forks, colliders, and d-separation; covers the effects of interventions, including the adjustment formula, backdoor and front-door criteria, inverse probability weighting, mediation, and causal inference in linear systems; and advances to counterfactuals, their structural interpretation, probabilities, graphical representation, and practical applications such as attribution, hiring discrimination, and personal decision making.

**Abstract**:

The Book of Why is a popular-science introduction to the "Causal Revolution" led by computer scientist Judea Pearl and science writer Dana Mackenzie. It explains how causality has been transformed from a philosophical mystery into a rigorous mathematical science, tracing the history of causal inference from early statistical taboos against discussing causation through path analysis, Bayesian reasoning, and causal diagrams to modern tools such as the do-calculus, confounding adjustment, and counterfactual reasoning. Organized around the "Ladder of Causation" (observation, intervention, and counterfactuals), the chapters cover confounding and deconfounding, the smoking-and-cancer debate, statistical paradoxes, mediation analysis, and the implications of causal reasoning for big data and artificial intelligence.

| **Keywords** | causal inference, counterfactuals, randomized experiments, observational studies, confounding, inverse probability weighting, g-formula, marginal structural models, target trial emulation, causal mediation |

**Abstract**:

Causal Inference: What If is a textbook aimed at health and social scientists for making explicit, assumption-driven causal inferences from data. It emphasizes articulating the causal question and separating the roles of data and assumptions. The book is organized in three parts of increasing difficulty: (I) causal inference without models, covering causal effects, randomized and observational studies, effect modification, interaction, causal diagrams, confounding, selection bias, measurement bias, and random variability; (II) causal inference with models, including IP weighting, marginal structural models, standardization and the g-formula, G-estimation, propensity scores, instrumental variables, survival analysis, and machine-learning-based variable selection; and (III) causal inference for time-varying treatments, covering treatment strategies, treatment-confounder feedback, g-methods for longitudinal data, target trial emulation, and causal mediation.
